# Kernel-only Lean 4 proof of C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 25/19

This directory holds a second Lean 4 + Mathlib proof of the bound C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 25/19 of Section 6 of the paper for the
signed third autocorrelation constants, from the same certificate as `../lean_25_19`
(`one_sided_tau6_25.json.gz`, τ = 6/25, sha256 2a76bd9b523ac8991bf99c5a8a00e9eefca84b867dc2a1b558cfa2d3eaa686d8, in the
Zenodo record). In `../lean_25_19` the finite certificate checks are evaluated by `native_decide`. Here every check is a
`decide +kernel` proof, evaluated by Lean's kernel, so the three theorems depend only on the three standard axioms. The
proof relies on Lean's kernel, including its built-in arithmetic on natural numbers, instead of code generated by Lean's
compiler.

Theorem 1.3 of the paper states C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 4/3 and rests on another certificate, which has no Lean proof; this
directory proves the weaker bound 25/19. Where a docstring in `src/` calls a statement "the main theorem of the paper", it
means Theorem 1.3 with 25/19 in place of 4/3. Docstrings in `src/` also number some lemmas differently from the paper;
the renumbering is described in `../lean_25_19/README.md`.

## Statements (`src/C64/Defs.lean`)

The theorems `KData.mainStatement : C64.MainStatement`, `KData.mainStatementEss : C64.MainStatementEss` and
`KData.constStatement : C64.ConstStatement` (in `src/kmain/KData/Main.lean`) have the statements of `../lean_25_19`,
word for word: `src/C64/Defs.lean` is the same file.

* `C64.MainStatement`: for every `f : ℝ → ℝ` that is integrable and zero almost everywhere outside `[−1/4, 1/4]`, with
  `∫ f ≠ 0`, every real number `M` with `(f ⋆ f)(t) ≤ M` for almost every `t` satisfies `(25/19) (∫ f)² < M`.
* `C64.MainStatementEss`: for the same `f`, with essential suprema in `EReal`:
  `(25/19) (∫ f)² < ess sup (f ⋆ f) ≤ ess sup |f ⋆ f|`.
* `C64.ConstStatement`: `25/19 ≤ C₃′ ≤ C₃`, where `C₃′` (and `C₃`) is the supremum in `EReal` of the constants `C` such
  that every admissible `f` and every real almost-everywhere upper bound `M` of `f ⋆ f` (of `|f ⋆ f|`) satisfy
  `C (∫ f)² ≤ M`.

## How the proof works

* `src/C64/` (the formalized argument and the soundness proof of the checker) and `src/C64Core/` (the checker) are
  byte-identical to `../lean_25_19/src/C64/` and `../lean_25_19/src/C64Core/`. `src/C64K/` (the kernel port and its
  simulation lemmas) is byte-identical to `../lean_kernel/src/C64K/` (the 30/23 kernel-only tree), and `src/C64Shard/`
  is the same as there except `LeafK.lean`, which adds the radius parts below.
* `src/C64K/` is a port of the checker for evaluation in the kernel (core Lean: `KBall`, `KNum`, `KChk`, `KDec`). It
  works with natural numbers and signs, on which the kernel's arithmetic is fast. Its simulation lemmas (Mathlib:
  `KBallSim`, `KNumSim`, `KChkSim`, `KChkSim2`) prove that it computes the same results as the checker:
  `C64KChk.checkArc_eq : ∀ g a, C64KChk.checkArc g a = C64Chk.checkArc g a` and
  `C64KChk.checkGlob_eq : ∀ g, C64KChk.checkGlob g = C64Chk.checkGlob g`.
* `src/C64Shard/` splits the check of an interval into pieces small enough for the kernel, since its memory grows with
  the size of a single declaration.
  * The leaf tree of an interval is cut into units.
  * A unit with a large witness leaf is cut further into parts: the exact checks and the crossing radius, the winding
    number, segments of ten tile-arc records, and the last segment.
  * The crossing radius r′ of such a unit is computed in advance by `tools/rpass.py`. One theorem (`uRB`) proves that
    the radius function returns r′. The segment parts (`uSegR`, `uLastR`) use r′ as a literal, and `seg_of_R` /
    `last_of_R` combine them with that theorem. A wrong literal can only make a theorem fail.
  * Each piece is its own theorem. Lemmas proved once and for all (`unit_of_parts`, `seg_step`, `seg_of_R`,
    `last_of_R`, `groups_join`, `arc_of_groups` and others) combine the pieces into the check of the whole interval.
* `src/tools/` writes the Lean data from the certificate.
  * `split_cert.py` and `gen_lean_data.py` are the tools of `../lean_25_19`. `gen_lean_data.py` sets the working
    precision of the checker's interval arithmetic to P = 80 bits (64 for the 30/23 proofs), since at P = 64 some
    tile-arc records of this certificate are too close to a sign boundary to decide; see `../lean_25_19/README.md`.
  * `gen_kshards.py` writes the kernel-only modules. `rpass.py` computes the radii r′ (`rprime.json`), which are not
    trusted. `gen_kmain.py` writes the main module.
* The generated modules, in `KData/`:
  * `Glob.lean`: the global data.
  * `A<i>.lean`, one per interval of `T1`: the interval's data. For an interval of type E it also proves the check.
  * `A<i>_G<k>.lean`: the piece theorems of the interval, by `decide +kernel`.
  * `A<i>_Ok.lean`: `C64KChk.checkArc glob A<i>.arc = true`.
  * `GlobOK.lean`: `C64KChk.checkGlob glob = true`.
  * `src/kmain/KData/Main.lean`: transfers the checks to the checker with the simulation lemmas, checks by
    `decide +kernel` that the intervals cover [0, 1] (`cover_ok`), then applies the soundness theorem of `src/C64/`.
* The statements do not mention the certificate, so the generators need not be trusted.

## Axioms

Each of the three theorems depends on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` only. `axioms_clean.txt` is the
output of `Axioms.lean` (`#print axioms` for the three theorems). The Lean sources in `src/` and the modules written by
`gen_kshards.py` and `gen_kmain.py` contain no `native_decide`, `implemented_by`, `extern` or `sorry`.

## Independent check

Coverage: a seeded 10% of pieces kernel-checked independently; the rest, and the final std-3 axiom sets, rest on the
producer's build.

* All 41,353 generated modules and `Main.lean` regenerate byte-identically from the certificate.
* A seeded sample of 3,671 piece modules was compiled from the regenerated sources, and Lean's kernel checked every
  `decide +kernel` proof in them; those theorems use only the standard axioms.
* With the piece proofs replaced by placeholders, all 2,104 assemblies and `Main.lean` build, and the statements of the
  placeholders equal those of the compiled pieces.
* `rprime.json` is not trusted: each r′ is pinned by its `uRB` theorem. As a control, a one-bit change of a radius r′ is
  rejected by the kernel.

## Building

Requirements: elan (`src/lean-toolchain`), Mathlib `v4.35.0-rc3` (commit c55e6e786f49, `src/lake-manifest.json`) and
Python 3 with `python-flint` 0.9.0. Run the steps in `src/`, with the certificate `one_sided_tau6_25.json.gz` from the
Zenodo record in that directory.

1. Split the certificate:

       python3 -I tools/split_cert.py one_sided_tau6_25.json.gz SPLIT

2. Write the kernel-only modules with the shipped radii, in 48 parts of 50 intervals each (the parts may run in
   parallel), and merge them into `KData/`. `Glob.lean` and `GlobOK.lean` are the same in every part:

       for k in $(seq 0 47); do
         a=$((50 * k)); b=$((50 * (k + 1))); [ $b -gt 2374 ] && b=2374
         python3 -I tools/gen_kshards.py SPLIT OUT/p$k --arcs $(seq -s, $a $((b - 1))) --checker k --scale 0.4 \
             --module 900 --rprime ../rprime.json
       done
       mkdir -p KData && cp -n OUT/p*/KData/*.lean KData/
       cmp KData/Glob.lean OUT/p0/KData/Glob.lean && cmp KData/GlobOK.lean OUT/p0/KData/GlobOK.lean

   To recompute the radii instead, run step 4 (the libraries) first, then:
   * run the loop of step 2 with `OUT0` in place of `OUT` and without `--rprime`;
   * copy only `Glob.lean` and the data modules into `KData/` and compile them (the first lines of step 5, with
     `LEAN_PATH` exported as there):

         mkdir -p KData && cp OUT0/p0/KData/Glob.lean KData/
         for d in OUT0/p*; do cp $d/KData/A[0-9][0-9][0-9][0-9].lean KData/; done

   * write, run and collect the radius files:

         python3 -I tools/rpass.py gen RP OUT0/p*/kplan.json --batch 8
         for f in RP/RP/*.lean; do lean --root=RP "$f" > "${f%.lean}.out"; done
         python3 -I tools/rpass.py collect RP

   * then run step 2 with `--rprime RP/rprime.json` and go on with step 3. The data modules and `Glob.lean` already in
     `KData/` are the same as the ones step 2 writes, and their compiled modules can stay in `kbuild/`.

3. Check the modules against `kdata_sha256.txt`:

       (cd KData && sha256sum -c ../../kdata_sha256.txt | grep -vc ': OK$')     # 0
       (cd KData && ls | LC_ALL=C sort | xargs cat | sha256sum)                  # 7641e346…

4. Build Mathlib, the checker, the port, the glue and the soundness proof (the library `C64Shard` has no root module,
   so its modules are built by name):

       lake exe cache get
       lake build C64Core C64Core.Load C64K C64Shard.ShardK C64Shard.LeafK C64.Check.SoundMain

5. Compile the generated modules in three rounds, `JOBS` at a time: the data, then the piece theorems and `GlobOK`, then
   the assemblies. A module needs up to about 5 GB of memory. Every command must exit with 0:

       export LEAN_PATH=$(lake env printenv LEAN_PATH):$PWD/kbuild LEAN_NUM_THREADS=1
       mkdir -p kbuild/KData && lean -o kbuild/KData/Glob.olean KData/Glob.lean
       for pat in '^A[0-9]{4}$' '^(A[0-9]{4}_G[0-9]{3}|GlobOK)$' '^A[0-9]{4}_Ok$'; do
         ls KData | sed 's/\.lean$//' | grep -E "$pat" | xargs -P JOBS -I{} lean -o kbuild/KData/{}.olean KData/{}.lean
       done

6. Regenerate `kmain/KData/Main.lean` (sha256 543e330d…, as shipped) from the 48 plans of step 2, and compile it:

       python3 -I tools/gen_kmain.py 2374 kmain "6 / 25" OUT/p*/kplan.json
       lean --root=kmain -o kbuild/KData/Main.olean kmain/KData/Main.lean

7. Print the axioms and compare them with `axioms_clean.txt` (the standard three for each theorem):

       cp ../Axioms.lean . && lean Axioms.lean

Resources: the 41,353 modules (Glob, 2,374 data modules, 36,873 piece-theorem modules, 2,104 assemblies and GlobOK)
took about 13,900 CPU-hours in total and 3.2 hours of wall-clock time on 12 servers with 384 threads each. The slowest
module took about 37 minutes. `Main.lean` took seconds on one server.
