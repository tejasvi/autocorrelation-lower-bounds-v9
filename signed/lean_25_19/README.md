# Lean 4 proof of C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 25/19

This directory holds a Lean 4 + Mathlib proof of the bound C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 25/19 of Section 6 of the paper for the signed
third autocorrelation constants, from the certificate `one_sided_tau6_25.json.gz` with τ = 6/25 (sha256
2a76bd9b523ac8991bf99c5a8a00e9eefca84b867dc2a1b558cfa2d3eaa686d8, in the Zenodo record). Theorem 1.3 of the paper states
C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 4/3 and rests on another certificate, which has no Lean proof; this directory proves the weaker bound 25/19.
Where a docstring in `src/` calls a statement "the main theorem of the paper", it means Theorem 1.3 with 25/19 in place
of 4/3. Docstrings in `src/` also number some lemmas differently from the paper: their Lemma 6.3 is Lemma 6.2, their
Lemma 6.4 is Lemma 6.3 and their Lemma 6.6 is Lemma 6.4; Lemma 6.5, Definition 6.6, Proposition 6.7 and Corollary 6.8
agree.

C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 25/19 is verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib; the 2,375 finite certificate checks are evaluated by
`native_decide` (one auxiliary axiom per check); an independent rebuild re-checked every module in Lean's kernel, and
the exported proof, with the auxiliary axioms taken as given, is accepted by `leanchecker` and by two other kernel
implementations, nanoda and lean4lean (see below).

A kernel-only proof of the same statements, which relies on Lean's kernel instead of code generated by Lean's compiler,
is in `../lean_kernel_25_19`. The 30/23 bound has proofs of both kinds, in `../lean` and `../lean_kernel`.

## Statements (`src/C64/Defs.lean`)

* `C64Data.mainStatement : C64.MainStatement`. For every `f : ℝ → ℝ` that is integrable and zero almost everywhere
  outside `[−1/4, 1/4]`, with `∫ f ≠ 0`, every real number `M` with `(f ⋆ f)(t) ≤ M` for almost every `t` satisfies
  `(25/19) (∫ f)² < M`.
* `C64Data.mainStatementEss : C64.MainStatementEss`. For the same `f`, with essential suprema in `EReal`:
  `(25/19) (∫ f)² < ess sup (f ⋆ f) ≤ ess sup |f ⋆ f|`.
* `C64Data.constStatement : C64.ConstStatement`. `25/19 ≤ C₃′ ≤ C₃`, where `C₃′` (and `C₃`) is the supremum in `EReal`
  of the constants `C` such that every admissible `f` and every real almost-everywhere upper bound `M` of `f ⋆ f` (of
  `|f ⋆ f|`) satisfy `C (∫ f)² ≤ M`.

Here `f ⋆ f` is Mathlib's convolution with respect to Lebesgue measure. The theorems have no further hypotheses.

## How the proof works

* `src/C64/` formalizes the argument of Section 6 of the paper in winding-number form, without the argument principle or
  the Jordan curve theorem, and proves the checker sound. It uses only the three standard axioms. It is the tree of the
  30/23 proof in `../lean` with two files changed:
  * `C64/Defs.lean`: the constant 25/19 in the three statements, and docstrings;
  * `C64/Check/SoundMain.lean`: τ = 6/25 in the hypothesis of `mainStatement_of`, and the constant 25/19 = 1/(1 − 6/25)
    in the proofs.

  `src/C64Core/` is the same as there. In `src/tools/`, `gen_lean_data.py` sets the working precision P = 80 and
  `gen_main.py` differs in a docstring. The generated `C64Data/Glob.lean` and `C64Data/Main.lean` carry the data of this
  certificate.
* `src/C64Core/` is the checker (core Lean only, compiled to native code): it re-derives every condition of the
  certificate from the definitions, with exact arithmetic and interval enclosures. Its working precision P is a
  parameter of the data (`Glob.P`), and the exporter sets it to 80 bits (64 for the 30/23 proof). At P = 64 the enclosure
  of S(z_c) is too wide to decide the sign condition Re(conj(v) S(z_c)) ≤ 0 for tile-arc records of this certificate
  whose v is within about 10⁻¹⁶ (relative) of that boundary; the closest is 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁷ away (comment in
  `src/tools/gen_lean_data.py`).
* `src/tools/` translates the certificate into Lean data: `C64Data/Glob.lean` with the global data and one module
  `C64Data/A<i>.lean` for each of the 2,374 intervals of `T1`.
  * Each arc module proves `checkArc C64Data.glob A<i>.arc = true` by `native_decide`. For an interval of type E that
    is the exclusion along the arc; for one of type H it is the crossing localization and every leaf of its tree.
  * `Glob.lean` proves `checkGlob glob = true`: the parameters, and the odd winding number of `S` along `T1`.
  * `C64Data/Main.lean` assembles these into the three theorems. It also checks, by `decide +kernel` (`cover_ok`),
    that the intervals cover [0, 1].
  * The statements do not mention the certificate, so the exporter need not be trusted.

## Axioms

Each of the three theorems depends on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` and 2,375 `native_decide` auxiliary
axioms, one per interval of `T1` and one for the global checks. `native_decide` makes the Lean compiler part of the
trusted base. `axioms_clean.txt` is the output of `Axioms.lean` (`#print axioms` for the three theorems).

## Independent check

An independent rebuild regenerated the data from the certificate: the concatenation of the modules, `Glob.lean` and
`Main.lean` agree with `data_sha256.txt` and the shipped files. It rebuilt every module from scratch and re-checked each
one in Lean's kernel. The three theorems have exactly the types `C64.MainStatement`, `C64.MainStatementEss` and
`C64.ConstStatement`, and depend on the three standard axioms and the 2,375 `native_decide` auxiliaries listed above.

The exported proof of the three theorems (11,721 definitions, 35,825 theorems, 2,378 axioms: the 2,375 `native_decide`
auxiliaries and the standard three) is accepted by `leanchecker` and by two other kernel implementations, nanoda (with
the auxiliaries permitted by name) and lean4lean. A corrupted export is rejected by all three.

* `source_sha256.txt`: the sha256 of every source file in `src/`. The comment in `src/lakefile.toml` that mentions

## Building

Requirements: elan (`src/lean-toolchain` pins `leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0-rc3`), Mathlib `v4.35.0-rc3` (commit
c55e6e786f49, in `src/lake-manifest.json`), and Python 3 with `python-flint` 0.9.0. The exporter uses `python-flint` to
evaluate the crossing radius of the certificate in ball arithmetic at 80 bits (a precision of its own, also 80 bits for
the 30/23 proof, independent of the checker's P). Run the steps in `src/`, with the
certificate `one_sided_tau6_25.json.gz` from the Zenodo record in that directory.

1. Split the certificate into `SPLIT/glob.json` and `SPLIT/arcs/0000.json` to `2373.json`, sorted by `s0`.
   `glob.json` holds the variant, τ, `T1`, the tiles, the number of intervals and the sha256 of the input:

       python3 -I tools/split_cert.py one_sided_tau6_25.json.gz SPLIT

2. Write the Lean data: `C64Data/Glob.lean` and one module `C64Data/A0000.lean` to `A2373.lean` per interval. Parallel
   processes with `--range a:b` over disjoint ranges give the same output, for example eight processes with
   `a = 297k`, `b = min(297(k+1), 2374)`, `k = 0..7`:

       python3 -I tools/gen_lean_data.py SPLIT . --modules

3. Check the data against `data_sha256.txt`:

       ls C64Data/A*.lean | LC_ALL=C sort | xargs cat | sha256sum    # 13bcc5c609563e4763a673058d75ecd5bd959ca2a12a6c3c1072d89c136c1402
       sha256sum C64Data/Glob.lean                          # a1b05aae605098ab540b821bc6a165c659c63615baa01ccd430a73d8b1a09b02

4. Build Mathlib, the checker (compiled to shared libraries under `.lake/build/lib/lean`) and the soundness proof:

       lake exe cache get
       lake build C64Core C64Core.Load C64.Check.SoundMain

5. Build the 2,375 data modules, `JOBS` at a time. The logs go to `results/shards/<module>.log`, whose last line is
   `OK ms` or `FAIL ms`. The last line of output is the number of failed modules, which must be 0:

       tools/build_shards.sh JOBS

6. Regenerate `C64Data/Main.lean` (sha256 2e9068c58a9db2bfbed5896c2640cfdd1f1ca83df41bf87ebf55b32d1fd15b0f, as
   shipped) and compile it (a few seconds):

       python3 -I tools/gen_main.py 2374 . "6 / 25"
       D=.lake/build/lib/lean
       lake env lean --load-dynlib=$D/LeanC64_C64Core_Ival.so --load-dynlib=$D/LeanC64_C64Core_Num.so \
           --load-dynlib=$D/LeanC64_C64Core_Check.so -o $D/C64Data/Main.olean C64Data/Main.lean

7. Print the axioms and compare them with `axioms_clean.txt`. Each of the three theorems depends on `propext`,
   `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` and exactly 2,375 `native_decide` auxiliary axioms (`C64Data.globOK` and
   `C64Data.A0000.ok` to `C64Data.A2373.ok`):

       cp ../Axioms.lean . && lake env lean Axioms.lean

Resources:
* Disk: about 30 GB at the peak. `SPLIT` holds 9.5 GB of JSON and can be deleted after step 2; Mathlib and the
  toolchain take about 10 GB.
* Memory: a data module needs well under 2 GB while it compiles, and `Main.lean` a few GB.
* Time: the 2,375 data modules took about 124 CPU-hours in total and 27 minutes of wall-clock time on one server
  running 282 modules at a time. The slowest one took about 19 minutes.
