# Kernel-only Lean 4 proof of C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 30/23

This directory holds a second Lean 4 + Mathlib proof of the bound C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 30/23 for the signed third
autocorrelation constants, from the same certificate as `../lean` (`one_sided_tau7_30.json.gz`, τ = 7/30, sha256
1a530af38c24b53f9bd5b0b183964d871eacce999e64f0487655b04342b94f18, in the Zenodo record). In `../lean` the finite
certificate checks are evaluated by `native_decide`. Here every check is a `decide +kernel` proof, evaluated by Lean's
kernel, so the three theorems depend only on the three standard axioms. The proof relies on Lean's kernel, including its
built-in arithmetic on natural numbers, instead of code generated by Lean's compiler.

Theorem 1.3 of the paper states C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 4/3 and rests on another certificate, which has no Lean proof. The bound
C₃ ≥ C₃′ ≥ 25/19 of Section 6 has Lean proofs in `../lean_25_19` and `../lean_kernel_25_19`; this directory proves the
weaker bound 30/23. Docstrings in `src/` use other numbers than the paper: Theorem 6.1 in `src/C64/Defs.lean` and
"Theorem 1.3" in `src/kmain/KData/Main.lean` both mean Theorem 1.3 of the paper with 30/23 in place of 4/3. The
renumbering of the lemmas is the one described in `../lean/README.md`.

## Statements (`src/C64/Defs.lean`)

The theorems `KData.mainStatement : C64.MainStatement`, `KData.mainStatementEss : C64.MainStatementEss` and
`KData.constStatement : C64.ConstStatement` (in `src/kmain/KData/Main.lean`) have the statements of `../lean`, word for
word: `src/C64/Defs.lean` is the same file.

* `C64.MainStatement`: for every `f : ℝ → ℝ` that is integrable and zero almost everywhere outside `[−1/4, 1/4]`, with
  `∫ f ≠ 0`, every real number `M` with `(f ⋆ f)(t) ≤ M` for almost every `t` satisfies `(30/23) (∫ f)² < M`.
* `C64.MainStatementEss`: for the same `f`, with essential suprema in `EReal`:
  `(30/23) (∫ f)² < ess sup (f ⋆ f) ≤ ess sup |f ⋆ f|`.
* `C64.ConstStatement`: `30/23 ≤ C₃′ ≤ C₃`, where `C₃′` (and `C₃`) is the supremum in `EReal` of the constants `C` such
  that every admissible `f` and every real almost-everywhere upper bound `M` of `f ⋆ f` (of `|f ⋆ f|`) satisfy
  `C (∫ f)² ≤ M`.

## How the proof works

* `src/C64/` (the formalized argument and the soundness proof of the checker) and `src/C64Core/` (the checker) are
  byte-identical to `../lean/src/C64/` and `../lean/src/C64Core/`.
* `src/C64K/` is a port of the checker for evaluation in the kernel (core Lean: `KBall`, `KNum`, `KChk`, `KDec`). It
  works with natural numbers and signs, on which the kernel's arithmetic is fast. Its simulation lemmas (Mathlib:
  `KBallSim`, `KNumSim`, `KChkSim`, `KChkSim2`) prove that it computes the same results as the checker:
  `C64KChk.checkArc_eq : ∀ g a, C64KChk.checkArc g a = C64Chk.checkArc g a` and
  `C64KChk.checkGlob_eq : ∀ g, C64KChk.checkGlob g = C64Chk.checkGlob g`.
* `src/C64Shard/` splits the check of an interval into pieces small enough for the kernel, since its memory grows with
  the size of a single declaration.
  * The leaf tree of an interval is cut into units.
  * A unit with a large witness leaf is cut further into parts: the exact checks and the crossing radius, the winding
    number, segments of ten tile-arc records, and the last segment.
  * Each piece is its own theorem. Lemmas proved once and for all (`unit_of_parts`, `seg_step`, `groups_join`,
    `arc_of_groups` and others) combine the pieces into the check of the whole interval.
* `src/tools/` writes the Lean data from the certificate. `split_cert.py` and `gen_lean_data.py` are the tools of
  `../lean`. `gen_kshards.py` writes the kernel-only modules and `gen_kmain.py` the main module.
* The generated modules, in `KData/`:
  * `Glob.lean`: the global data.
  * `A<i>.lean`, one per interval of `T1`: the interval's data. For an interval of type E it also proves the check.
  * `A<i>_G<k>.lean`: the piece theorems of the interval, by `decide +kernel`.
  * `A<i>_Ok.lean`: `C64KChk.checkArc glob A<i>.arc = true`.
  * `GlobOK.lean`: `C64KChk.checkGlob glob = true`.
  * `src/kmain/KData/Main.lean`: transfers the checks to the checker with the simulation lemmas, then applies the
    soundness theorem of `src/C64/`.
* The statements do not mention the certificate, so the generators need not be trusted.

## Axioms

Each of the three theorems depends on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` only. `axioms_clean.txt` is the
output of `Axioms.lean` (`#print axioms` for the three theorems). The Lean sources in `src/` and the modules written by
`gen_kshards.py` and `gen_kmain.py` contain no `native_decide`, `implemented_by`, `extern` or `sorry`.

## Independent check

All 15,663 generated modules were rebuilt from scratch on independent machines, with Lean's kernel checking every
module. They were built from regenerated sources whose SHA-256 agree with `kdata_sha256.txt`. The types of the three
final theorems were compared with the statements above (equal as expressions), and they use only the standard axioms.

Exports of parts of the proof were also given to `leanchecker` and to two other kernel implementations:
* `leanchecker`, nanoda and lean4lean accepted the soundness chain (`main_of_checks`, `mainStatement_of`,
  `mainStatementEss_of`, `constStatement_of`), `checkArc_eq` and `checkGlob_eq`, `GlobOK` and the interval A1193
  (type E).
* `leanchecker` and nanoda also accepted A0000 (its two piece modules and its assembly). The lean4lean run on A0000 was
  not completed.

The complete export, about 5.7 million constants, was not checked by a second kernel.

## Building

Requirements: elan (`src/lean-toolchain`), Mathlib `v4.35.0-rc3` (commit c55e6e786f49, `src/lake-manifest.json`) and
Python 3 with `python-flint` 0.9.0. Run the steps in `src/`, with the certificate `one_sided_tau7_30.json.gz` from the
Zenodo record in that directory.

1. Split the certificate (as in `../lean`):

       python3 -I tools/split_cert.py one_sided_tau7_30.json.gz SPLIT

2. Write the kernel-only modules, in eight parts of 329 intervals each (the parts may run in parallel), and merge them
   into `KData/`. `Glob.lean` and `GlobOK.lean` are the same in every part:

       for k in 0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7; do
         a=$((329 * k)); b=$((329 * (k + 1))); [ $b -gt 2626 ] && b=2626
         python3 -I tools/gen_kshards.py SPLIT OUT/p$k --arcs $(seq -s, $a $((b - 1))) --checker k --scale 0.4
       done
       mkdir -p KData && cp -n OUT/p*/KData/*.lean KData/
       cmp KData/Glob.lean OUT/p0/KData/Glob.lean && cmp KData/GlobOK.lean OUT/p0/KData/GlobOK.lean

3. Check the modules against `kdata_sha256.txt`:

       (cd KData && sha256sum -c ../../kdata_sha256.txt | grep -vc ': OK$')     # 0
       (cd KData && ls | LC_ALL=C sort | xargs cat | sha256sum)                  # 09fa4e2f…

4. Build Mathlib, the checker, the port, the glue and the soundness proof:

       lake exe cache get
       lake build C64Core C64Core.Load C64K C64Shard.ShardK C64Shard.LeafK C64.Check.SoundMain

   The library `C64Shard` has no root module, so its modules are built by name as above.

5. Compile the generated modules in three rounds, `JOBS` at a time: the data, then the piece theorems and `GlobOK`, then
   the assemblies. A module needs up to about 5 GB of memory. Every command must exit with 0:

       export LEAN_PATH=$(lake env printenv LEAN_PATH):$PWD/kbuild LEAN_NUM_THREADS=1
       mkdir -p kbuild/KData && lean -o kbuild/KData/Glob.olean KData/Glob.lean
       for pat in '^A[0-9]{4}$' '^(A[0-9]{4}_G[0-9]{3}|GlobOK)$' '^A[0-9]{4}_Ok$'; do
         ls KData | sed 's/\.lean$//' | grep -E "$pat" | xargs -P JOBS -I{} lean -o kbuild/KData/{}.olean KData/{}.lean
       done

6. Regenerate `kmain/KData/Main.lean` (sha256 c719dcce51a2f0e0a86ddac233929b86d29fe67e22913be1510565554df8ef21, as
   shipped) from the eight plans of step 2, and compile it (seconds):

       python3 -I tools/gen_kmain.py 2626 kmain "7 / 30" OUT/p0/kplan.json OUT/p1/kplan.json OUT/p2/kplan.json \
           OUT/p3/kplan.json OUT/p4/kplan.json OUT/p5/kplan.json OUT/p6/kplan.json OUT/p7/kplan.json
       lean --root=kmain -o kbuild/KData/Main.olean kmain/KData/Main.lean

7. Print the axioms and compare them with `axioms_clean.txt` (the standard three for each theorem):

       cp ../Axioms.lean . && lean Axioms.lean

Resources: the workers built 13,278 of the modules: `Glob`, the 2,626 data modules, the 10,649 piece-theorem modules,
`GlobOK` and one assembly. They took about 11,300 CPU-hours in total and 6.4 hours of wall-clock time on 24 servers with
96 threads each. The slowest module took about 2.5 hours. The other 2,385 assemblies and `Main.lean` took minutes on one
server.
